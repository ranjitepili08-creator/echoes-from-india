# Indian Organology & Acoustic Synthesis Guide

Indian classical musicology divides all musical instruments into four fundamental families (*Chaturvidha Vadya*) according to Bharata Muni's *Natya Shastra* (c. 200 BCE):

1. **तत वाद्य (Tata Vadya) — Chordophones (Strings)**
   - Characterized by stretched silk, gut, or wire strings over wooden/gourd resonators.
   - Distinctive feature: *Jivari* (buzzing flat bridge) producing rich harmonic overtone dispersion.
   - Examples: Yazh, Pinaka Veena, Rudra Veena, Saraswati Veena, Ravanahatha, Mayuri Veena.

2. **सुषिर वाद्य (Sushira Vadya) — Aerophones (Wind)**
   - Sound excited by breath turbulence and air column vibration.
   - Examples: Algoza (twin continuous-breathing fipple flutes), Shankha (sacred conch horn).

3. **अवनद्ध वाद्य (Avanaddha Vadya) — Membranophones (Drums)**
   - Hollow wooden/clay barrels covered with tuned parchment membranes.
   - Dual-head tuning: Left bass head with moist wheat dough (*Aata*), Right treble head with permanent black iron-ore tuning paste (*Syahi*).
   - Examples: Pakhawaj, Mridangam, Mardala.

4. **घन वाद्य (Ghana Vadya) — Idiophones (Solid Resonators)**
   - Resonates without membrane or string tension.
   - Examples: Jal Tarang (water-tuned porcelain bowls struck with bamboo mallets).
